# Cedar Observatory

Cedar Observatory sits on Cedar Bluff above the harbor, far enough inland that spray does not reach the dome. Dr. S. Okonkwo opened it in 1902 and hired local apprentices to sit the night watches.

The principal telescope is a 12-inch reflector whose mirror was figured by H. Pell. Mara Ellison logged 214 meteors at the Cedar observatory during the August 1908 Perseid watch. She counted between 11 p.m. and 3 a.m. and signed the page in the red observing book.

Cedar observatory retired barometer number 17 after the glass cracked in the January 1912 freeze. The instrument was a mercury barometer and was not repaired. The Cedar observatory dome slit faces 12 degrees west of true north. That offset keeps the lighthouse beam out of the eyepiece.

In 1905 the public was admitted on Thursday evenings, two hours after sunset. The Thursday rule did not apply to apprentices, who held keys year round.
