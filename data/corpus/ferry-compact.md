# Ferry Compact

The harbor authority and the Pell & Rowe Ferry Company wrote the ferry compact to settle night fares, freight hours, and who would judge a dispute. Copies are kept in the clerk's cabinet and in the company safe at Pell Wharf.

The ferry compact was signed on April 3, 1894. The ferry compact sets the night crossing fare for a horse cart at 40 cents. A foot passenger paid 12 cents on the same night boats. The ferry compact opens the dawn freight window at 4:10 a.m. Ice and fish have that window to themselves until 5:30 a.m.

Clause 7 requires a spare hawser of manila rope, at least 80 fathoms, stored in locker B. The ferry compact names Judge Inez Calder of the county court as arbitrator. The compact was renewed in 1910 and gained a Sunday stop at Gull Wharf. Sunday boats still collect the weekday fare.

The company painted its boats grey so coal dust would show. Grey paint is not part of the fare schedule.
