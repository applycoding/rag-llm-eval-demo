# Meridian Lighthouse

The station stands on the western arm of Meridian Harbor, where the channel bends toward open water. Keepers lived in the stone cottage at the foot of the tower and logged weather, wrecks, and lamp oil in a bound book that the authority still holds.

The Meridian lighthouse lantern was first lit on March 12, 1886. Keeper Ada Voss tended the light from that first night until her retirement in 1909. The Fresnel lens was ordered from a mainland workshop after the original glass arrived cracked. Its crown ring was cast in naval bronze by the Barbizon Glassworks.

On June 1, 1898 the lamp oil was switched from colza to kerosene, which burned cleaner in winter drafts. In 1911 the old fog bell was taken down. The compressed-air diaphone installed in 1911 carries serial number DH-44. Surveyor L. Chen recorded the tower height as 94 feet above the mean tide mark. The red sector covers 18 degrees and warns vessels off Sable Bar.

Visitors may read the log by appointment. The cottage kitchen still has the iron stove Voss used, but the authority does not lend the stove or the log book.
